# Design

## What it is read off

Photos' editor on iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, driven in the simulator by a UI test that opened a photo and a clip, entered Edit and stepped through each mode, keeping a screenshot and the element tree of each step (Photos' own names: `edit.tool.adjust`, `Enhancements`, `Intensity Slider for Auto`, `Aspect Ratio`).

- **A phone, upright.** At the top, on glass, 44-point buttons from the top of the safe area, 16 in from the sides and 12 apart: Cancel (✕) alone, Undo and Redo on one capsule (52 apart); More (···) and Done (✓, yellow once there's something to keep) on the right. The status bar and home indicator are hidden. Under the picture, everything measured up from the mode bar (62 high, 16 off the bottom, the modes 46.8 apart): the ruler 71 above its top, the tools 113 (51-point cells, 71 apart), the name's capitals centred 152 above it (160 over the filters), 15.5-point rounded, grey.
- **An iPad.** The status bar shows; the top bar is the same 44-point buttons, 10 in, with Show Original first on the history capsule and the mode's name in the middle (14-point medium, secondary label). The modes down the left (a 76.5-wide capsule, 64 apart, the mark 18.5 in, pointing at the chosen one); the ruler 75 in from the right, 43 from the top and bottom, turned a quarter; the tools 117 in, 86 from them, shown 361 points each way from the middle; the name's right edge 36 before the tools. Photos also has Zoom and Markup there.
- **Adjust.** Auto, Exposure, Brilliance, Highlights, Shadows, Contrast, Brightness, Black Point, Saturation, Vibrance, Warmth, Tint, Sharpness, Definition, Noise Reduction, Vignette: each a 19-point symbol on a 51-point face with a faint ring (Auto a larger wand on grey, dark once on); a value is an arc round it, yellow the clock's way for more, dark the other way for less, and its number replaces the symbol from the first touch of the ruler until another tool is chosen. A tool switched off (its cell tapped again) is faint and its ruler dimmed; moving the ruler switches it back on. Its ruler (`CEKSlider`): 41 marks 9 apart over the whole range, every tenth darker; the mark at the value stands up yellow (dark while the ruler is off); a dot marks the default while the value is away from it.
- **Filters.** Original and nine looks as thumbnails on a wheel (`CEKWheelScrubberView`): on a phone 52-point squares 54 apart on a wheel of 200, three each way whole and the fourth turning grey as it fades; on an iPad 51 points, 53 apart, on a wheel of 700, shown as far as the tools. The one in the middle is under a fixed white frame; a dot marks the one applied. Each filter keeps its own amount; the ruler goes for Original. While the amount moves, the frame darkens under it in percent, and the bars (top and modes) hide until it's let go, as they do while the filters are dragged.
- **Crop.** Undo and Redo give way to Flip and Rotate (a quarter turn left) on the capsule, and More shares its capsule with Aspect Ratio. The picture sits in a canvas from 20 under the top bar to 141 over the mode bar (on an iPad 141 in from each side and 125 off the bottom), in a box with 3-point corners 20 long, 25-point bars on each edge and a 1-point line, all just outside it; corners and edges are 44-point handles, 7 and 17 in. Outside the box the picture is dimmed to half and blurred (`systemThinMaterial`); while the box or the picture moves, the blur goes, a grid of thirds shows and the bars fade, and a second after the finger lifts the box grows back into the middle on a critically damped spring (stiffness 300). Straighten (45° at 100%), Vertical and Horizontal (30°) are three tools on one ruler, read in degrees, positive turning counter-clockwise, taking the top back and bringing the right nearer; the tool's name shows on a capsule for a second and a half as it's chosen. Flip turns the picture over about the box's middle, a quarter turn swings it onto its new box. RESET (on the accent, under the top bar; on an iPad in its middle) shows while anything but the turns and flip is changed. Aspect Ratio opens in place of the tools on a phone (along the bottom on an iPad, where Wallpaper isn't offered): upright and level, then Original, Freeform, Square, Wallpaper and six ratios, the chosen one on a grey capsule; a ratio locks the box (a lock in its corner, ASPECT RATIO LOCKED for a moment) until Freeform or Lock Aspect Ratio in More frees it.
- **A clip** (after). A Video mode first, with mute on the top bar and a trimmer: play, the clip's frames between two handles, the playhead; in the other modes a small player over the picture.
- **More (···).** Copy Edits, Paste Edits, Save as Duplicate, Appearance, Markup, Extensions, Undo, Redo.
- **Its colours follow the device.** White in light, black in dark: no longer the black it always was.

Hangar has those, Adjust and Filters first, laid out to the same measures. What's Photos' own and not public (its tools' maths, Clean Up, Extensions) it does its own way or not at all.

## An edit is values

`HangarEdit` holds a value for each adjustment, the ones switched off, a filter and how much of it: never pixels. The picture is drawn from it every time (`HangarRenderer.apply`), every adjustment that applies in the toolbox's order, then the filter mixed in by its amount. So an edit opens again where it was left, a step back is the edit before, Copy Edits copies values to another picture, and an app keeps the edit beside its original as Photos does. It decodes whatever it lacks as untouched, so an edit kept now opens in a later version.

## The tools

Each is a `HangarAdjustment` (an id, a name, a symbol, a range, and what a value does to a `CIImage`) or a `HangarFilter` (a look). Photos' are made of Core Image's filters, close to Photos' in what they do and how far they go at their ends; curves (contrast, brightness, black point, raising highlights) are done on the picture as it's seen (sRGB's curve) rather than as light adds up, so they look as they're drawn. Vignette is a soft radial ring multiplied in (darker) or screened in (lighter): Core Image's vignette, done in linear light, brightened the whole picture at -1. The tests check each one's direction (warmer is yellower, tint up is magenta, a vignette darkens the corners) and that each keeps the picture's size; `ContactSheet` draws every tool at both ends and every filter on a photo, to look at while tuning them.

An app's tools are the same kind of thing as Photos': the toolbox is a list it can add to, take from and reorder. A tool works at any size: `context.scale` is how many of the full picture's pixels a pixel being drawn is, for a tool working over a distance; Definition and Vignette measure by the picture instead.

## Modes

Each mode is a module (`HangarModule`), Photos' three as much as an app's: the editor has no Adjust or Crop of its own. It keeps what every mode shares, the top bar, the mode bar, the picture drawn from the edit, the name over the tools, undo and flying in and out, and gives each mode a host to work through (`HangarHost`): the edit, `render()` and `commit()`, its places in Photos' layout (`HangarLayout`: the top bar's buttons, the mode bar, Adjust's tools and ruler, the picture's region), the bars to hide, the canvas to hide for a stage of its own. Crop is the one that asks most of it, a stage over the canvas, Flip and Rotate where Undo and Redo are, RESET where the iPad's title is, its own More, the editor flying home from its box: so whatever a mode of an app's needs, Crop has needed already. A mode chosen is shown in the editor's cross-dissolve (`show`), then told (`entered`); the one left is told after (`left`, Crop flying its box back to where the canvas will show it). After every change the edit didn't get from it (undo, Paste, Revert, a step made), each is synced (`sync`).

## Steps made of pixels

What a job makes (`HangarJob`: a model's, Vision's) can't be values, so an edit keeps it as a step (`HangarStep`): the ids of the pictures it made, in the app's store (`HangarAssets`), the one it uses, where it goes over the picture before it (0 to 1 each way; past the edges for more of the scene round it) and where it's kept (a mask). The steps make the picture everything else is drawn on (`HangarRenderer.base`): laid in turn over the original, each taking its frame's place (clear where it's clear: a background taken out stays out), at its own pixels (a picture made twice as large makes the whole twice as large, the rest scaled to it); then the geometry, the adjustments and the filter as always. So a step never has to be made again when anything after it changes, and anything after it can still change; a picture made larger keeps the crop on the same part of it. The editor's preview is the steps' picture at the screen's size, made again off the main thread when the steps change (undone, pasted, a step made). A job is given the picture as the steps make it (as large as it asks), shimmers over the part it works on, and Cancel stops it; what it makes is kept in the store and added as one step, one undo.

## The shape of a picture

`HangarGeometry` is the picture's shape as values too: Straighten, Vertical and Horizontal as angles, the crop as a part of the picture as it comes (0 to 1 each way), the ratio it's held to, quarter turns left and a flip. It's done in that order: the picture tilted as a camera turned would see it (a 28 mm lens for its diagonal: Vertical and Horizontal turn about its middle, which stays where it was), straightened, cropped, then turned and flipped. So the crop is drawn in the picture's own frame, and a turn or a flip never moves it; the crop is always inside the tilted picture (`HangarFit`), and an edit kept before Crop opens with none. Crop's canvas (`CropStage`) shows the picture turned and flipped, drawn once without the shape and moved by a 3D transform made from the same sums, so a ruler or a handle never redraws it.

## Drawing

The picture is drawn by Core Image into a Metal layer the picture's shape (`CanvasView`) each time the edit changes: a ruler dragged redraws it every frame. It's drawn as Photos' is (its `NUMediaViewRenderer` draws for the view's size and coalesces its renders): the source is the picture scaled once to the canvas's pixels and kept on the GPU (`insertingIntermediate`), so a frame is only the tools' work on the pixels shown; and a picture is made and drawn on a queue of the canvas's own, one at a time, a change that comes meanwhile taking the place of any still waiting. So the main thread never waits for the GPU (the ruler stays with the finger however long a frame takes), and what's drawn next is always the latest. The full picture is drawn when it's asked for (`HangarResult.render`), off the main thread, in the picture's own colours.

On an iPhone, the picture follows at 120 Hz only if the app has `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone` in its Info.plist (the example has); an iPad needs nothing. In a debug build, `HANGAR_SCRUB=<tool>` in the app's environment sweeps that tool's ruler for four seconds once the editor is in and writes how the frames went (the screen's, the picture's, the GPU's time on each) to Documents/scrub-<tool>.json; `HANGAR_SCRUB_ALL=1` puts every other tool a third of the way first.

## Moving things

The ruler and the strips are scroll views: they're dragged and flicked as everything else on iOS, and come to rest on a value or a tool. A strip chooses what passes its middle as it goes (a tap felt for each), and a tap on a tool brings it there. A finger that stops a flick and lifts settles it there (a strip on the nearer one).

The ruler only ever changes the tool it was set to, as in Photos: while the tools move (a finger or a tap's quarter-second slide) it's off, stopped where it was and dimmed, and it's set to the tool they come to rest on. Its ends fade in a window that stays still over the sliding marks. It holds at the default as Photos' does (its snapping: 5 points, slower than 30 points a second): a finger moving slowly within 5 points of it is held there until it has gone 5 points past. A tap is felt at each mark crossed; the mark leaving the value sits down on Photos' spring, dark at first. A finger lifted slower than 100 points a second leaves it where it is; faster, it slides on. The tools strip taps once a tool is nearly in the middle (at once when flicked faster than 1500 points a second); the filters tap as each is chosen, once well past halfway.

The picture flies in and out on Flight (`FlightGroup`): from the source's frame to the canvas and back, its target read every frame. Flying in, the page under it is covered within the first third of the way, as the picture leaves its place; flying home, it shows through only as the picture is about to land (the cube of the way), so the picture never sits beside a copy of itself.

## Its look

`HangarStyle` is the editor's roles, not Photos' colours: the background, words and symbols, the name's grey, a mode not chosen, the accent (the chosen mode's mark, the ruler's mark at its value), the raised colour (a tool's ring and number above its default), a tool's face, Auto's faces, the default's dot, the fonts, glass or not. Each was read off Photos' pixels in light and dark. Photos' are the system's, following the device's light and dark; an app gives its design's tokens for the same roles.
